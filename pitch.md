## Hook (30s)

I want to start this presentation with a question to the audience: when we walk around the world & use our senses, how many bits of information per second do we receive? 

There are different estimates using various methods, but neurophysiologists estimate that our human body transmits between 10 million bits and 1 billion bits per second. So, my question to you is: What do you think, how much of that information do our brains actually process?

The answer is actually less than 50 Bits per second! So our Brains only processes a vanishingly small percentage of the information our bodies transmits. 

We thought; Something where Computers and particularitly AI models excell at is at their capability to process an insane amount of data in a really short amount of time. So why not use it to make visible what our brain hides? 

Of course, this filtering process happens for a very good reason: Overall brain activity needs to be regulated, and we need to discard information as not get overstimulated.  So we wanted to tackle the following questions in our project.

- How can use AI to process spatial information?
- How should we make the information available to the user?
- How long can an AI provide information before it starts talking nonesense, hallucinating?
- How can we make turn this idea to an interesting experience for the user, underlied with spatial music?

## How it works (1-2 min)

Our project is built on the WebXR framework and hosted on vales website and can be directly accessed with your phone. But of course, you get the intended experience when you open the browser in the meta quest 3 virtual reality headset. 

We connect the software with a local LLM that runs on Leans laptop. What happens is that when the user gazes on an object long enough, the LLM will get promted to do research on the object and present its finding to the user in audio form. If the user then pans their gaze to something else, the explanation will stop.
